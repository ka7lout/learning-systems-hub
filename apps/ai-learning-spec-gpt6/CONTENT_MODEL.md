# Content Model

## Source labels

Each curricular record has one primary category: `Original Curriculum`, `Harvard College`, `Harvard Extension`, `Industry Extension`, or `Research Extension`. Secondary mappings are explicit links, never category blending.

## Evidence statuses

`confirmed_current`, `confirmed_historical`, `likely_not_verified`, `not_found`, `design_decision`, and `research_hypothesis` are distinct values. The current verification date and official URL are stored with Harvard references. A current course tag does not guarantee term availability or a public syllabus.

## Node types

- `module`: one of the eight original modules or an additive extension spine.
- `unit`: a preserved numbered/original group.
- `lesson`: a named topic with objectives, content, notes guidance, assessment and prerequisites.
- `course_reference`: an official Harvard College or Extension mapping, not a claim of enrollment.
- `learning_science_configuration`: principles plus evidence and hypothesis boundaries.

## Lesson record

A lesson provides an objective, a why statement, a small example, an attempt-first task, a progressively useful hint, a changed-context transfer prompt, and notebook advice. `MUST WRITE` is compact: own explanation, essential rule/formula, one example, common mistake, why/when. Never transcribe a complete lesson.

## Mastery evidence

Completion, competence and evidence remain separate. A viewed page is not a mastered skill. Evidence may include recall, explanation, application, transfer, debugging, project artifacts, oral defense and delayed retrieval. Assistance level and independence are stored separately.

## Content ownership and licenses

The original topic outline is learner-provided content. Harvard pages are referenced by citation and link; source text/media are not copied. Only reuse external assets when their redistribution license allows it and attribution is retained.
